# 12 - Project, Leadership, and Behavioral Interviews

## Project Explanation Structure

1. Business problem and users
2. Scale and non-functional requirements
3. Your exact responsibility
4. Architecture and critical data flow
5. Hardest constraint and alternatives considered
6. Failure/security/consistency design
7. Testing, rollout, and observability
8. Measured outcome
9. Incident or mistake and learning
10. What you would change now

## Prepare Evidence Stories

- difficult production incident
- performance improvement with measurements
- security/reliability defect prevented
- disagreement resolved with evidence
- migration delivered incrementally
- mentoring and team leverage
- failed decision and correction
- ambiguous project clarified
- cost reduction without reliability loss
- cross-team standard adoption

## STAR Without Losing Technical Depth

- Situation: concise context and stakes
- Task: your ownership and constraints
- Action: decisions, alternatives, implementation, communication
- Result: metrics, user/business effect, learning

## Red Flags to Avoid

- saying “we” without identifying your contribution
- claiming zero failures or no disagreements
- blaming another team
- presenting tools as outcomes
- giving scale without units or evidence
- hiding tradeoffs
- describing only the happy path
- inventing metrics or ownership

## Senior Closing Questions

Ask about system constraints, engineering quality signals, incident ownership, architectural decision-making, deployment frequency, platform boundaries, security expectations, and how success is measured for the role.
